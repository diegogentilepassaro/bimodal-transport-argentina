RECONCILIATION (cr-review PR #143, blocking 2). Read literally on
p-values the outcome is the SECOND scenario: neither ma47_ctrl
(p = 0.139) nor ma60_ctrl (p = 0.118) rejects at 10%. But the
second scenario's CONCLUSION does not follow, for three reasons
that the p-values alone hide:
  (a) Near-collinearity. The two levels correlate 0.9993 raw
      and 0.9989 after the full47 controls, so this contrast
      NEVER had the power to separate them. Removing the post-1947
      population content changes nothing because there was almost
      nothing to remove -- which is itself the answer to whether
      'MA 1960 is post-outcome' can justify dropping it.
  (b) The coefficient, not the p-value, is what moves. With any
      baseline-MA control it is +0.0966 (t7), +0.0913 (pop47),
      +0.0837 (1947 wts), +0.0872 (1960 wts); without one,
      -0.0062. ma47/ma60 sit above 0.10 because log pop 1947
      is a noisier baseline (SE 0.0459 -> 0.0564), not because
      the association went away.
  (c) Two confounds in the with-vs-without comparison, so it is
      suggestive and not a test. The CIs overlap:
      ma47_ctrl [-0.0269, +0.1942] vs full47 [-0.0770, +0.0645].
      And the first stage differs (F 11.3 with vs 23.4
      without): the baseline-MA control absorbs instrument
      variation, the mechanism documented for the main spec in
      .kiro/baseline_ma_control_note.md (13.6 vs 29.9 there).
  NET: the post-outcome justification for dropping the baseline-MA
  control is UNSUPPORTED -- on (a), not refuted by (b)/(c). The
  defensible reading is pop47: keep the airtight fix (log
  pop 1960 is a component of the DV), keep the MA baseline,
  and report beta = +0.0913, p = 0.093 as a marginal rejection
  at 10%, not as a clean null.

INTERPRETATION LIMIT: conditioning on a lagged level or growth
