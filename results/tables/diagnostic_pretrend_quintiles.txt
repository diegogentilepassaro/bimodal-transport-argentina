[5] THE POPULATION BLOCK IS NOT A ROBUSTNESS RESULT
==========================================================================
  C2 and C3 bin on log_pop_1960, which carries the
  locality-universe measurement error documented in
  diagnostic_pop1960_universe.R and is also the initial level
  inside the outcome chg_log_pop_91_60, so they bin on a
  mismeasured variable whose error is shared with the outcome.
  C1 IS THE LEAST CONTAMINATED OF THE THREE, NOT EXEMPT. An
  earlier version of this note claimed it carried none of the
  objection, which is wrong: baseline MA is built FROM
  pop_1960, so binning on it bins on a tau-weighted sum of the
  same mismeasured variable. The error is missing dispersed
  rural population, hence rural-concentrated and spatially
  correlated, so a district's baseline MA inherits a
  neighbour-weighted version of an error correlated with its own.
  What genuinely attenuates the channel is that the MA sum
  excludes the district itself (04_market_access.R), so a
  district is never binned on its own mismeasured population.
  Attenuated, not closed (cr-review PR #165).

READING, in order of evidential weight
==========================================================================
  [1] LINEARITY IS REJECTED IN SEVERAL PLACES. At the five
  percent level the baseline-log-MA dummies are jointly
  significant for 2 of 6 outcomes and the baseline-log-pop
  dummies for 4 of 6. The two are not the same outcomes: in
  baseline MA it is establishments (p = 0.0089) and population
  (p = 0.0320); in baseline population it includes the two
  manufacturing outcomes the paper leans on, production value
  p = 0.0239 and wage mass p = 0.0013, and population at p = 0.0000.
  So the honest statement is NOT that the linear form is fine
  where the paper needs it. It is rejected there too, in the
  population baseline.

  [1] AND [2] TOGETHER ARE THE POINT, and they point the same way
  for a reason worth stating. A control's functional form being
  rejected is not the same thing as the coefficient of interest
  being biased by it. Wage mass is the cleanest case: linearity
  in baseline population is rejected at p = 0.0013, and relaxing it
  moves the IV-Both estimate by 7% (C2). Production value:
  rejected at p = 0.0239, moves 5%. The misspecification is
  real and it is orthogonal to the estimand.

  [3] SAYS NO CHANGE IS DISTINGUISHABLE FROM ZERO. The smallest
  bootstrap p across the six outcomes is 0.057 (Mfg. establishments),
  and every 95% interval contains zero. Population, the largest
  percent change at 42%, has p = 0.150 and an interval of
  [-0.062, +0.006]. The percent changes are large mainly because
  the denominators are small: every IV-Both estimate in the two
  blocks that move is insignificant under every specification.
  An earlier version of this script reported those percentages
  alone and read them as a finding.

  WHICH COLUMN MOVES. For population the strong column moves
  least: IV-LP +0.052 to +0.040 against IV-Hypo +0.080 to +0.019.
  The mix shifts too: the LP first stage weakens (23.28 to 18.29)
  while the hypothetical one strengthens (4.00 to 7.39), so
  IV-Both under C1 leans more on the weak instrument than under
  C0. That makes IV-Both a less clean comparison than it looks,
  which is a second reason to read [1] instead.

  WHAT THIS STILL CANNOT SETTLE. Quintiles are one departure from
  linearity. [1] tests that departure and no other.
  There are 16 distinct first stages here, not 24: outcomes that
  share a sample share a first stage.

  the theta switch to 4.14 and is not the C0 row here.
