  The partial correlation is a SCREENING RULE, not a test. A high
  value means the swap has little room to move anything, so a
  similar coefficient carries no information. A low value would
  not by itself make the comparison decisive.
  theta_low  (4.140): partial corr 0.9907
  theta_high (8.110): partial corr 0.9958

  BOTH CALIBRATIONS ARE ABOVE THE 0.95 GATE, so the check is
  near-uninformative throughout. IV-B moves +0.059 -> +0.052 at
  theta_low and +0.024 -> +0.023 at theta_high, and NEITHER is
  evidence that the pop_1960 universe problem fails to reach the
  estimate through the baseline control: there was almost nothing
  to remove in either case. This is the position PR #143 reached
  on the 1947-population version, and unit weighting does not
  escape it. The check belongs in the record, not in the paper as
  a robustness result.

  WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED to answer the question: a baseline control
  that keeps the convergence role while carrying variation the
  population-weighted level does not. Unit weights do not deliver
  that, and neither did 1947 population. Both are still
  distance-decay sums over the same 1960 tau, and that shared
  structure is what drives the correlation.

  SCOPE. This addresses only the CONTROL channel. The outcome's
  1960 denominator and the log_pop_1960 control carry the same
  measurement error and are untouched here, so nothing above
  speaks to those two.
  UNIT WEIGHTS ARE NOT POPULATION-FREE IN EFFECT, only in
  construction; see [3] above.
  The 0.95 threshold is a reporting convention agreed with the
  coauthors, not a test: nothing here computes a p-value for
  collinearity, and the cut is arbitrary at the margin.
